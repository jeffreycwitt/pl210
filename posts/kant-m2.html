On p. 12, Kant identifies the second proposition of morality (or second proposition of what it means to have a good will).
First, what was the 1st proposition (see the footnote on p. 12)?
Second, what is the 2nd proposition?
What does Kant mean by a “maxim” (see the footnote on p. 13)?
What’s the difference between “a formal principle of volition” and “a material principle of volition”?
Don’t get distracted by Kant’s fancy words. Think about these two principles in relation to an action being done for a particular effect versus action being done for its own sake. Which one of these actions has a formal principle? And which one has a material principle?
What is the third principle of morality (p. 13)?
How does it follow from the first two? (Or perhaps rather than think about how it “follows,” think about how this is really another way of saying the same thing.)
On page 15, Kant gives a generic formulation of the law. It is famously known as the “Categorical Imperative” (see footnote 15). Think about why this formula—as a formula that all laws must in some way express if they are going to be legitimate laws—might follow from the principles of morality that Kant has already stated.
In other words, if I really have a good will, why would I only be adopting maxims (i.e. willing) that can be willed universally?
