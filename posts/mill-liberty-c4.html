What does Mill think is the strongest objection to the arguments for interference in the lives of individuals (p. 634b)? What does this say about collective or universal reason?
I am particularly interested in your reaction to Mill’s answer to the objection posed in the second paragraph on p. 632b-633a. His answer seems to come primarily on p. 634a and suggests that the downfall of individuals left alone to harm themselves without intervention is a “cost” that is outweighed by the greater good of freedom for those who can use it well.
What do you think of this answer? Do you think society should be willing to leave some people behind in order to ensure the freedom of others? Or do you think society has a responsibility to care for the weakest even if this brings about inconveniences for others? How much inconvenience to others can be tolerated?
Are there contemporary examples of this dilemma? Such as modern gun laws? Why? What other examples come to mind?
