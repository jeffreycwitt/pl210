<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>Consider how "nature" and "art" are described and related in the opening lines.</Question>
<Question level={2}>When he says "nature" what does he mean?</Question>
<Question level={2}>How is art related to nature? How is it similar to nature or different?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>Why is the Leviathan or the State said to be a product of art? How is it an imitation of nature?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>As the State is described, what kind of picture of the human person emerges?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>At the bottom of 117b Hobbes gives a description of his plan for the rest of his book. What does he plan to do?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>Regarding the first part of his investigation, he criticizes where others have begun their investigation and instructs us to start elsewhere. Where does he think we should begin?</Question>
<Question level={2}>Does this tell us anything about his procedure?</Question>
<Question level={2}>Is there something distinctively modern about this starting point?</Question>
<Question level={2}>Could he be rejecting the methods of the past in some way? If so, how?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

While I'm not asking you to read chapters 1-4, it is a good idea to look at the titles of these chapters and browse around to get a sense of what Hobbes is up to.

</QuestionBlock>
