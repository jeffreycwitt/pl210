Leviathan, cc. 17-19

What is the end or purpose of the commonwealth (c. 17)?
How does this connect to Hobbes' discussion of the need for covenants and the difficulty of making a "valid" covenant in c. 14 and 15?
Why can't human beings be like bees (c. 17)?
What is the "scientific" definition of a commonwealth (c. 17)?
How does the commonwealth come into existence?
Is the covenant that produces the commonwealth a "valid covenant"?
If so, why is this covenant valid, while prior to this covenant, "valid covenants" seemed impossible? In other words, what is unique about this covenant (c. 17)?
What is distinctive about a commonwealth that comes to be through an act of Instituting (c. 18)?
How might this differ from other aggregates of people or factions?
What are the consequences of such an Institution (i.e. what rights belong to the Sovereign)?
Consider one or two of the rights of the Sovereign and why this right necessarily follows from the nature of the Instituted commonwealth (c. 18)?
Why must the dissenting vote also recognize the elected sovereign (c. 18)?
At the close of chapter 18, Hobbes notes that the reader might complain that life under the Sovereign seems very miserable. First, having read this far in his book, what is your reaction to the society he describes? Is it a society you would want to live in? Why or why not? Second, how does his response to the objection affect your own opinion? Is he right that we complain only because we have on "multiplying glasses" rather than "prospective glasses"?
