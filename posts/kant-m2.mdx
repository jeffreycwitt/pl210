<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>Second, what is the 2nd proposition?</Question>
<Question level={2}>What does Kant mean by a “maxim” (see the footnote on p. 13)?</Question>
<Question level={2}>What’s the difference between “a formal principle of volition” and “a material principle of volition”?</Question>
<Question level={3}>Don’t get distracted by Kant’s fancy words. Think about these two principles in relation to an action being done for a particular effect versus action being done for its own sake. Which one of these actions has a formal principle? And which one has a material principle?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<Question level={2}>How does it follow from the first two? (Or perhaps rather than think about how it “follows,” think about how this is really another way of saying the same thing.)</Question>
</QuestionBlock>

<QuestionBlock type="reading-content">
<Question level={1}>On page 15, Kant gives a generic formulation of the law. It is famously known as the “Categorical Imperative” (see footnote 15). Think about why this formula—as a formula that all laws must in some way express if they are going to be legitimate laws—might follow from the principles of morality that Kant has already stated.</Question>
<Question level={2}>In other words, if I really have a good will, why would I only be adopting maxims (i.e. willing) that can be willed universally?</Question>
</QuestionBlock>
